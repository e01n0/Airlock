Airlock speaks with recorded clips when a voice pack is installed — every cue is a short audio file, so the coach sounds like a person and works offline. How much the coach says
Full counts you through the long phases and adds a coaching line on every hold. Normal calls each phase and coaches now and then. Sparse calls the phase and nothing else. Silent leaves the talking to the tones.

Chime
A tone at every phase change, synthesised in the browser — no download, always works offline. It rises into an inhale and falls into an exhale, so you can pace with your eyes shut and the phone face down.
Guide tone
A continuous tone that glides up through the inhale and down through the exhale, and rests flat on a hold. Follow it instead of the screen.
Count the last five

CO₂ tolerance
The BOLT score (Body Oxygen Level Test) is how long you can comfortably hold after a normal, relaxed exhale — stopping at the first definite urge to breathe, not at your limit. It tracks how sensitive your body is to CO₂, and it is the number this app is built to move. Measure it first thing in the morning, before coffee and before any breathing practice, for a number you can compare week to week.

Scale sessions to my score
On, hold and breathe-up lengths in the tables are computed from your latest BOLT instead of fixed numbers, so the work stays honest as your tolerance climbs. Off, the tables use the durations you dial in.

SafetyRead this
  • Never in or near water. Breath-holding before or during swimming causes shallow-water blackout and it is the single most common way apnea training kills people. Not in the bath, not in the pool, not on a paddleboard.
  • Never while driving, cycling, standing on stairs, or operating anything.
  • Sit or lie down. Holds and fast breathing can make you faint. A faint on the floor is a nuisance; a faint standing up is an injury.
  • Stop at once if you feel dizzy, get pins and needles that don’t settle, see spots, or your chest hurts. Breathe normally and rest.
  • Talk to a doctor first if you are pregnant, or have epilepsy, high blood pressure, heart or lung disease, a history of fainting, panic disorder, or an aneurysm.
  • Air hunger is meant to be uncomfortable. Pain, panic and losing control are not the training — back off the numbers.
  • Airlock is a training timer, not medical advice or a medical device.
